# Metadata (META)

### META-01 · Every indexable page has a unique title of a sensible length · high
- **Check:** crawler `title-missing`, `title-long` (low, over 60 characters: Google sets "no limit on how long a `<title>` element can be" and cuts the title link to fit, so 60 is our own rough line for where the end may not show), `title-short` (flagged under 20 characters; aim for about 30–60), `title-dup`, `title-multiple`. The short-title flag stays at 20 so that short, honest titles (a product name, a brand and one word) aren't flagged; between 20 and 30 is a judgement call, not a finding.
- **Width, not characters:** Google cuts a title link "as needed, typically to fit the device width" ("Influencing title links"), so both lengths are counted in Latin-letter widths. A Chinese, Japanese or Korean character counts as two (they're full-width), and a Thai or accent mark that sits on its letter counts as none. A 25-character Chinese title is as wide as 50 Latin letters. This rough count is our own, not Google's. The crawler and `content-scan.mjs` both count this way.
- **Pass:** unique, about 30–60 characters (some projects allow 65), main keyword first, brand once at the end.
- **Fix:** a title pattern per page type in the profile, e.g. tool pages: "DMARC Checker - Free DMARC Record Lookup | Brand". Where pages compete for one phrase, give that phrase to one page and retitle the others (CONTENT-04).
- **Site-wide:** when the title is missing, or one title is shared, on at least 80% of indexable pages (and at least 3), the crawler reports one `*-sitewide` finding as a **blocker**, not one high per page. Keep it a blocker: it's the usual sign of a site with no SEO work.

### META-02 · Every indexable page has a unique meta description of 120–160 characters · medium
- **Check:** crawler `desc-missing`, `desc-length`, `desc-dup`, and `desc-starts-with-title` (low): the description starts with the page's title, or its first 80%, for titles of 20 or more characters.
- **Pass:** says what the visitor gets and why this page, in a plain sentence that names the page's main keyword (CONTENT-03); no template text with a name swapped.
- **Fix:** add a description field to the content model, or generate one per page type from specific data, never from boilerplate.
- **Reflects the page:** `desc-mismatch` (low) flags a description that barely matches its page, read as META-09 reads a title: fewer than a quarter of its main words (4+ letters, not stopwords or brand words, found by their stem or as an acronym) are on the page. It needs 4 main words and a page of 50+ words; the quarter is our own line. Google uses a description only when it may describe the page better than the page's own text ("Control your snippets in search results"), so a mismatched one is likely replaced.
- **Brand tail:** `desc-brand-tail` (low) flags a description that ends with the same part the title ends with after a separator ("… | Acme"), usually the brand. The title shows right above it in results.
- **Templated:** `templated-desc` (medium), on 3 or more pages or items: descriptions that end in a stock call to action ("Learn more", "Read more", "Find out more", "Discover more", "Click here", "See more", "Call us today", "Contact us now", "Get in touch today", "Get started today", "Shop now", "Book now", "Order now"), or whose last sentence (3+ words, after another sentence) also closes 2 or more other descriptions. A description used word for word on several pages is `desc-dup`'s, so it isn't counted here. The crawler checks crawled pages; `content-scan.mjs` also flags descriptions that are only the title plus a few words.
- **Templated share:** with any templated descriptions, the crawler (`templatedDescriptions` in `seo-crawl.json`) and the content scan (`stats.templatedDescriptions`) give their share, and a verdict for the report's "Descriptions and keyword use" panel: **a cluster** when one section holds them (a URL's first folder, or content-scan's `--group`, where 3+ pages and at least half the section are templated, together 80% of them), **site-wide** when half the descriptions or more are templated, otherwise **scattered**. These lines are our own. The share is information only; the findings are what count.

### META-03 · All metadata comes from one helper · medium
- **Check:** crawler `title-brand-twice` (with `--brand`), and `duplicate-head-tags` (low): two or more meta descriptions, robots tags, viewports or `og:title` tags on one page. Repeated `og:image` tags are fine, as Open Graph reads them as a list (ogp.me). Without `--brand`, duplicate tags can fail META-03 but nothing passes it. Code: how pages set titles, canonicals, Open Graph and robots.
- **Pass:** one helper sets the title, description, canonical, Open Graph, Twitter card and robots. The title template doesn't double the brand.
- **Fix:** centralize it. When a page title already contains the brand, use an absolute title.

### META-04 · Open Graph and Twitter tags on every page; key pages get their own image · low
- **Check:** crawler `og-missing`: no `og:title` or `og:image`. Its detail also names `og:description` when the page has no meta description either; a page missing only a description is `desc-missing` (META-02), which has the same fix. `twitter-card-missing` (low): a share image but no `twitter:card`, so X shows at most its small card; set `summary_large_image` (a page with no share image gets `og-missing` only). `twitter-card-invalid` (low): a value other than `summary`, `summary_large_image`, `app` or `player`. X reads `twitter:card` from `name=` or `property=` and takes the title, description and image from the `og:` tags (X's cards markup and getting-started guides). `og:description` is optional on ogp.me, and Facebook, LinkedIn and X don't say they use the meta description in its place, so it's named only when both are missing. Code: does a per-page `openGraph` object replace the inherited image? (A common Next.js trap.)
- **Fix:** set a default image in the shared helper and give key page types their own. Prefer the framework's own generator (Next.js `opengraph-image.tsx`); otherwise make 1200×630 cards with `scripts/og-card.mjs` (no AI, no outside service). Always set `og:image:width`, `og:image:height` and `og:image:alt`. Use a real photo, not a text card, where the page has one. See `og-images.md`.
- **Crawler:** flags an `og:url` that differs from the page's canonical (`og-url-mismatch`).
- **Share images:** the crawler reads the first 64 KB of up to 20 og:images on the site itself (never a third-party host): `og-image-broken` (medium) when the URL doesn't return the image, `og-image-no-size` (low) when `og:image:width`/`og:image:height` are missing.

### META-05 · The page language is declared · low
- **Check:** crawler `lang-missing` on every indexable page, listing the pages whose `<html>` has no `lang`.
- **Fix:** set `<html lang>` in the root layout, and in any layout that replaces it.

### META-06 · Robots meta depends on page type, using the object form · medium
- **Check:** code: robots values in the metadata helper.
- **Pass:** indexable pages are `index, follow` with `max-image-preview:large` and `max-snippet:-1`. Search, filter and result pages are `noindex, follow`. Account, admin, sign-in and 404 pages are `noindex, nofollow`.
- **Fix:** derive it from the page type. A plain string robots value can silently override the layout's richer object.
- **Crawler:** flags a page whose robots meta and `X-Robots-Tag` header disagree on indexing (`robots-conflict`, high). Search engines follow the stricter of the two.
- **Nofollow:** `page-nofollow` (medium) flags an indexable page whose robots meta or header says `nofollow` (or `none`): Google follows none of its links. Rules are read as Google reads them (CRAWL-02).

### META-07 · Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools are verified · medium
- **Check:** code: verification meta or DNS TXT (ask the owner if you can't tell).
- **Fix:** verify a Domain property by DNS; Bing can import it from Search Console. Never ship placeholder verification codes.

### META-08 · hreflang only when there are real language or region versions · low
> Checked **21 Nov 2026** against Google's "Tell Google about localized versions of your page" and "Managing multi-regional and multilingual sites".
- **Check:** code: `alternates.languages`, `<link rel="alternate" hreflang>`, a `Link` response header, or `<xhtml:link rel="alternate" hreflang>` in the sitemap.
- **Pass:** each version lists all the others and itself, plus `x-default` (for language selectors and auto-redirecting home pages), with fully qualified URLs. Codes are ISO 639-1 language codes with an optional ISO 3166-1 region (`en-GB`); `UK`, `EU` and `es-419` are silently ignored. Each version canonicalizes to itself in the same language. Use one method only (HTML, header or sitemap).
- **Never:** auto-redirect by IP or `Accept-Language` (Googlebot usually crawls from the US with no `Accept-Language`). Use a banner suggesting the other version instead.
- **Fix:** remove one-sided or self-only hreflang that serves no purpose, unless the site deliberately targets one region.
- **Countries:** a country-code domain is Google's clearest country signal. hreflang marks language and region versions, and Google still uses it. Search Console's country targeting is gone (`page-types.md`).
- **Other sites:** with `--outside` and `--hreflang-other-sites`, hreflang targets on other sites are visited (20 at most) and must link back.
- **Our own other address:** an hreflang link to this site's own host over `http://`, or with or without `www`, isn't another site. It's `hreflang-bad-target` (high), because it points at an address that redirects or duplicates the page, not at the canonical one.
- **Crawler:** reads hreflang from all three places Google does (the HTML, the HTTP `Link` header and the sitemap's `<xhtml:link>`), merged per page so the same language and target in two places counts once. On the merged set it checks return links, a self entry, `x-default`, valid ISO 639-1 language and ISO 3166-1 region codes (`en-uk` is invalid; it's `en-gb`), and targets that are 200, indexable and canonical. URLs are compared by path, so relative links and slash variants match.
- **Sources:** Google treats the three alike and says using more than one brings no benefit. `hreflang-sources-disagree` (medium) flags a page whose sources conflict: the same code at different URLs, or the same URL under different languages (`x-default` may share a version's URL). Google doesn't say which one wins. A source that just lists fewer versions isn't flagged, since Google combines them.
- **Versions match:** `hreflang-missing-version` (low) flags a page that leaves out a version listed by the pages it links to, under both that code and that URL. A version on the site counts only when it was crawled and is indexable, so a page isn't told to link to one that isn't published; one on another host can't be checked and counts. `lang-hreflang-mismatch` (low) flags a page whose `<html lang>` has a different primary language than every code hreflang lists it under (`en` and `en-GB` agree; `nb`, `nn` and `no` count as one). Google ignores `lang` and goes by the visible text, but screen readers use it (WCAG 3.1.1).
- **Untranslated versions:** `untranslated-copy` (medium) flags two versions in different primary languages, linked by hreflang, that share 80% of their main text, or have the same title and share half of it. A title counts from five words, not counting words with digits (model numbers) or the site's brand words (in most of its titles), so a product name kept as the title in every language doesn't. Google works out a page's language from its visible text, so an untranslated version counts as the original language. `en-GB` and `en-US` may share their text.
- **Language values:** `lang-invalid` (low) flags a `lang` that isn't a BCP 47 tag (`auto`, `english`, `en_US`); an empty `lang` is `lang-missing` (META-05). `inlanguage-mismatch` (low) flags schema `inLanguage` values none of which share `<html lang>`'s language.
- **Translate widget:** `translate-widget` (low, once for the site) flags Google's Translate widget (its `translate_a/element.js` loader or the `goog-te-` classes) on a site where no page lists versions in two languages and every page's `lang` is the same language. It translates in the browser on the same URL, and Google recommends a separate URL per language version, so the translations don't reach search.

### META-09 · Each title matches what its page is about · low
- **Check:** crawler `title-mismatch`: an indexable page none of whose title's main words appear in its visible text. Main words have 4+ letters and aren't stopwords or brand words (words in more than half of the site's titles, plus `--brand`). A word counts when its stem is on the page ("pricing" finds "prices"), and an acronym when the page shows it in capitals: a capitalised word of the title ("LPG"), or the initials of three or more title words in a row, skipping and/of/the ("Frequently Asked Questions" finds "FAQ", "Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning" finds "HVAC"). The H1 is part of the page text, so a word or acronym it shares with the title counts.
- **Conservative by design:** it needs at least two main words and 50 words of page text, and skips words in scripts written without spaces (Chinese, Japanese, Thai). META-09 counts as checked only when at least one page had enough to compare.
- **Pass:** the title names what the page covers, in words its heading and text use.
- **Fix:** rewrite the title from the page's H1 and main topic. A mismatch usually means a copied template, a title left over from an older page, or the wrong field mapped to the title.

### META-10 · The site has a favicon search results can show · medium
> Checked **19 Nov 2026** against Google's favicon documentation (formats listed since 28 Aug 2026).
- **Check:** crawler, on the home page: its icon links (`icon`, `shortcut icon`, `apple-touch-icon`, `apple-touch-icon-precomposed`), then `/favicon.ico`, read only on the crawled site. The icon links are read from the `<head>`, or from the `<body>` when the head has none (Next.js can stream metadata there). `favicon-missing` (medium) when none loads; `favicon-unsuitable` (low) when the ones that load are all under 48 px, not square, or SVG or WebP only. An icon on another host (a CDN), or a `/favicon.ico` that redirects to one, isn't fetched, so META-10 is then listed under Limits.
- **Pass:** Google requires a square icon of at least 8×8 px and recommends one larger than 48×48 px, in BMP, GIF, ICO, PNG, JPEG, PPM or TIFF, at a stable URL that Googlebot-Image may crawl. Only one favicon per hostname.
- **Fix:** `<link rel="icon" href="/icon-192.png" sizes="192x192">` in the root layout, next to any SVG icon, plus a `/favicon.ico` for older tools.
