# Performance and Core Web Vitals (PERF)

> Thresholds checked **16 Nov 2026**, unchanged since INP replaced FID (March 2024): LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1, measured at the 75th percentile of real mobile visits. Claims that "good" LCP is now 2.0 s have no Google source: Google's Core Web Vitals page still says 2.5 s (updated 10 Dec 2025, read 22 Nov 2026).

> Google says only Core Web Vitals are used by its ranking systems; other page-experience factors (HTTPS, popups) "don't directly help your website rank". Rate those by their effect on visitors and crawling, not as ranking levers.

> AMP gives no ranking boost of its own. Google applies "the same standard to all pages, regardless of the technology used to build the page" (developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/amp), and since June 2021 AMP isn't needed for Top Stories and the AMP badge is gone (Search Central blog, 19 Apr 2021). Don't add AMP for rankings; a fast ordinary page does the same job. Checked 21 Nov 2026.

### PERF-07 · Popups don't block content or crawlers · high
- **Check:** code: modals, consent walls, age gates, app banners.
- **Pass:** no popup covers the whole page on arrival. Users aren't redirected to a separate consent or input page (Googlebot would see only that page). Required gates (age, legal) overlay the content instead of replacing it.
- **Crawler:** with outside checks on, `mobile-interstitial` flags a fixed layer covering half the phone screen or more about a second after load. Sticky elements, app shells (layers holding `<main>` or the H1) and small cookie bars don't count, and a consent tool's layer is a note, since legally required notices are exempt. Popups shown later or on scroll still need a manual look.

### PERF-01 · Core Web Vitals pass on mobile · high
- **Check:** field data (Search Console CWV report, PageSpeed Insights or CrUX) when the site is live; lab data (Lighthouse) before launch. The crawler can't measure CWV, so list it under `notChecked` unless you ran one of these.
- **Fix:** send real-user metrics to analytics with the `web-vitals` library, and fix the worst template first.
- **Data source:** with outside checks on, the crawler (`--outside`) fetches field data from PageSpeed Insights for the home page and one URL per section, up to 10, and flags a section whose 75th-percentile LCP, INP or CLS fails (`cwv-fail`). It needs a free PageSpeed Insights API key in `PSI_API_KEY`: Google refuses almost all keyless requests. Without the key, without outside checks, or before launch, PERF-01 stays under Limits.
- **Lab speed:** with outside checks on and Chrome or Edge installed, each sample page is also loaded once on a phone with 4× CPU slowdown and slow 4G. LCP, CLS and an approximate TBT go into the crawl as `labSpeed` and one Overview line labelled indicative. They aren't Lighthouse numbers and raise no findings. With the optional Lighthouse add-on and `--lighthouse`, up to 5 sample pages also get a real Lighthouse run, which is information too: lab data never passes or fails PERF-01; use them before launch, when there's no real-user data. `--lighthouse all` runs it on every indexable page (up to 200, about 20 seconds a page).
- **LCP breakdown:** the Overview names the biggest of LCP's four parts (server response, the wait before the main image starts loading, its download, drawing it) with the fix for that part. It comes from real visitors with `CRUX_API_KEY`, else from the home page's lab run.
- **Desktop:** with `--desktop` as well as `--outside` (its own yes: it sends the sample URLs to Google again), the same pages are asked of PageSpeed for desktop and, with a CrUX key, the desktop trend too. They go into `speed.desktop` and one Overview line. It's information only: no finding and no score, since PERF-01 is about phones.
- **Bands:** the phone and desktop lines give each real-user number Google's band: good up to LCP 2.5 s, INP 200 ms and CLS 0.1; poor above 4 s, 500 ms and 0.25; needs improvement in between (web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds, checked 20 Nov 2026).
- **More real-user detail** (information only, in the report's collapsed "Speed and image details"): the share of visits that were good, needing improvement and poor for each metric (PageSpeed's three distributions); FCP and TTFB with their own bands, which aren't Core Web Vitals (FCP good up to 1.8 s, poor over 3 s, web.dev/articles/fcp; TTFB good up to 0.8 s, poor over 1.8 s, "a rough guide", web.dev/articles/ttfb); and the period the data covers: PageSpeed's numbers are the last 28 days, and with a CrUX key the exact dates of CrUX's latest 28-day period.
- **Trend method:** with a CrUX key the crawl keeps the full weekly series (`speed.trend.series`, 25 weeks, a week with too few visits left as null), the 4-week averages of LCP, INP and CLS (the first four weeks with a value against the last four, steadier than two single weeks), and the dates of each period. It also asks CrUX for the home page alone (`speed.pageTrend`) and for tablets (`speed.tablet`, with its own LCP breakdown); both send only the site's address, like the phone trend, and are asked only once the phone trend came back.
- **Single-page apps:** real-user data is measured against the page the visitor first loaded. A route change inside the app (a "soft navigation") doesn't start a new page view, so LCP is the first page's and CLS and INP add up over the whole visit (web.dev/articles/vitals-spa-faq). Chrome began publishing experimental soft-navigation metrics in CrUX in October 2026, aiming to graduate them in May 2027; PageSpeed's numbers don't include them yet. Judge an app's inner pages with a lab run or your own `web-vitals` reporting.
- **The LCP element:** PageSpeed's and Lighthouse's lab runs name the largest element on each page they load (`media.heroImages`: the page, its label and, for an image, its file). `hero-no-fetchpriority` (PERF-03) uses Chrome's own check on that run.
- **Server response time:** every crawled page is timed from the request to its response headers (one request from this machine, so a lab number). `media.serverTimes` gives the median, where the slowest 5% start and the pages over 0.8 s, web.dev's "good" TTFB for real visitors. Information only: a local development server is often slow on a first request.
- **Lighthouse extras:** `--lighthouse 20` runs that many pages (the sample first, then the rest, up to 200). Each run keeps its Lighthouse version and its passed-audit count, and with two or more pages `media.lighthouseMedians` gives the median score per category. Lighthouse 12 (April 2024) removed the PWA category, so older reports with one aren't comparable. Chrome's CrUX Dashboard (Looker Studio) was deprecated after November 2025 (developer.chrome.com/blog/crux-dashboard-deprecation); CrUX Vis, built on the CrUX History API, is its replacement for looking at the weekly trend by hand.
- **Since last audit** says a page got slower or faster only when the change is real, and never compares one source with another (phone with desktop, or real visitors with a lab run):
  - Real visitors, phone and desktop each: a Core Web Vital crossed its limit, or moved 20% and at least 300 ms (LCP), 50 ms (INP) or 0.05 (CLS). Worse on any one of them counts as slower.
  - Lab load: LCP moved 40% and 1 s.
  - Lighthouse: the performance score moved 15 points.

### PERF-02 · Each page loads only its own code · medium
- **Check:** code: shared bundles importing every widget or heavy library; client components wrapping whole pages.
- **Fix:** dynamic import per widget, with server rendering kept on for anything with text.

### PERF-03 · The LCP element loads first · medium
- **Check:** crawler `lazy-lcp` (the first image has `loading="lazy"`, or a lazy-loading script's `data-src` behind a placeholder, as Perfmatters, EWWW, WP Rocket, LiteSpeed and lazysizes write it), `image-preloads` (low: more than one image preload on a page; preloads with a `media` attribute, one per screen size, count as one), `hero-no-fetchpriority` (low, with outside checks: in PageSpeed's or Lighthouse's lab run the LCP element is an image without `fetchpriority="high"`, Chrome's own "LCP request discovery" check), and `render-blocking` (low): a `<script src>` in the `<head>` without `async`, `defer` or `type="module"` (other script types, `nomodule`, scripts in comments and known consent managers such as Cookiebot, OneTrust, Usercentrics and Didomi don't count), or six or more stylesheets for every screen (no `media`, or `all`/`screen`; `<noscript>` fallbacks don't count). One or two stylesheets are normal and aren't flagged. And `img-not-lazy` (low): from the 11th image on, images with no `loading` attribute (an explicit `eager`, script-loaded `data-src` images and images under 100 px wide aside). web.dev advises `loading="lazy"` outside the first screen; the 11th image is this skill's working level, as the crawler can't see the layout. Code: hero components.
- **Pass:** the LCP is a real `<img>` (never a CSS background) or text. It's never lazy-loaded on any template, and gets `fetchpriority="high"` / framework `priority` (on its preload too, if it has one). At most one image preload per page, the hero's: web.dev says "avoid preloading too many resources. If too many resources are prioritized, effectively none of them are" (web.dev/articles/preload-critical-assets, checked 21 Nov 2026). Fonts use `font-display: swap` and are self-hosted or preloaded.
- **Other images:** `decoding="async"` lets the browser decode an image without holding up other content; web.dev says the effect "may only be noticeable on very large, high-resolution images" (web.dev/learn/performance/image-performance), so it's advice for big images below the hero, not a check.

### PERF-04 · Layout doesn't jump · medium
- **Check:** crawler `img-dimensions`. Code: banners injected after load, results areas that grow, embeds.
- **Pass:** images and embeds have width and height or an aspect-ratio box. Dynamic areas reserve space. Nothing is inserted above content after load.

### PERF-05 · Interactions stay fast · low
- **Check:** code: heavy work on input (parsing, hashing, large lists), and these patterns (web.dev/articles/optimize-inp, checked 21 Nov 2026):
  - **Long tasks:** one event handler doing everything. Break the work into separate tasks and yield to the main thread between them, doing only what updates the screen first.
  - **Layout thrashing:** changing styles in JavaScript and then reading a size or position in the same task, which forces the browser to lay the page out at once. Read first, then write.
  - **Synchronous storage and XHR:** `localStorage` and `sessionStorage` are synchronous and block the main thread (web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web); synchronous `XMLHttpRequest` outside workers is being removed from the web platform for the same reason (xhr.spec.whatwg.org). Use IndexedDB and `fetch`.
- **Fix:** debounce, move CPU work to a Web Worker, and show progress.

### PERF-06 · Documents and third parties stay light · low
- **Check:** crawler `html-size` (over 500 KB) and `dom-size` (over 1,400 elements in the body of the server HTML; scripts may add more). Code: third-party scripts and iframes (maps, chat, video).
- **DOM size:** 1,400 body elements is the level Lighthouse's "Avoid an excessive DOM size" audit flagged until Lighthouse 13 (October 2025). Its successor, the "Optimize DOM size" insight, flags slow style and layout work (over 40 ms) instead of a count, which is what large pages cause.
- **Depth and width** (information only): `media.domShape` lists pages nested more than 32 levels deep and elements with more than 60 children, read from the server HTML. These are our own lines, from older Lighthouse advice; Lighthouse itself never failed a page on them, and Chrome's insight docs say depth alone isn't a problem but can signal unneeded nesting. So they're leads for trimming wrappers and long lists, not findings.
- **Pass:** third-party domains kept to a minimum. Iframes load on interaction or below the fold and carry a descriptive `title`. Images are no more than about 2× their display size and use modern formats.

### PERF-08 · Images are a sensible weight and in a modern format · low
- **Check:** the crawler sends a HEAD request for up to 50 same-origin images on indexable pages, with a browser's `Accept` header. It also flags `img-no-srcset` (low): images over 1,000 px wide by their `width` attribute with no `srcset`, outside `<picture>` and SVG aside. web.dev says desktop-size images can cost phones 2–4 times the data; 1,000 px is this skill's working level.
- **Pass:** no image over 200 KB, and no JPEG or PNG over 100 KB where WebP or AVIF would serve. These are this skill's working thresholds, not Google's; Lighthouse's image audits measure the same thing as savings. An image over 500 KB is `image-over-500kb` (medium, our own line) instead of `heavy-image`. The images weighed include those behind a lazy-loading script's placeholder (their `data-src`).
- **JPEG XL:** Chrome ships JPEG XL decoding from Chrome 155 (developer.chrome.com/blog/jpeg-xl-in-chrome, 6 Oct 2026), after removing it in 2022. Not every browser and image tool has caught up, so serve it only inside `<picture>` with an AVIF, WebP or JPEG fallback; WebP and AVIF stay the safe default.
- **Caching** (information only): `media.imageCache` lists weighed images sent with no cache lifetime (`no-store`, `no-cache`, `max-age=0`, or no `Cache-Control` and no `Expires`), names the CDN when the image's headers show one (Cloudflare, CloudFront, Vercel, Netlify, Bunny, Azure Front Door, Fastly), and counts images on other hosts. Lighthouse flags static files that aren't cached and suggests a year (`max-age=31536000`) for files that don't change (developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/uses-long-cache-ttl). Not a finding, because a local server's headers rarely match production.
- **Fix:** resize to the displayed size, compress, and serve WebP or AVIF through the framework's image component or the CDN.
- **Savings and conversion:** the crawl lists the images it weighed as `images` (largest first, up to 100: bytes, pixel size, format, the pages using them and `estSaving`), and the report shows the top 15 in a collapsed "Images by possible saving" table, information only. `estSaving` is a rough estimate: WebP at the low end of Google's figures (lossy 25–34% smaller than JPEG, lossless 26% smaller than PNG, developers.google.com/speed/webp), plus resizing to 1,600 px wide, taken as cutting bytes in proportion to pixels. Where there's no image component or CDN to do it, `scripts/images.mjs --crawl seo-audit/seo-crawl.json --root <public folder>` converts the JPEG and PNG ones over 100 KB with sharp, the optional image add-on (`npx seo-supervisor --with-image-tools`, about 20 MB in `~/.seo-supervisor/tools`, never the project). It's a dry run until `--yes` and the owner's yes; it writes `.webp` (and `.avif` with `--avif`) next to the originals or under `--out-dir`, never overwrites or deletes a file, and prints the `<picture>` markup to use.

### PERF-09 · The next page starts loading before the click (speculation rules) · low
- **Check:** crawler `no-speculation-rules`: one suggestion when a site with more than one page has no `<script type="speculationrules">` on any page and no `Speculation-Rules` response header. Sites whose router already loads the next page itself (Next.js, Nuxt, Gatsby, SvelteKit, Remix, React Router 7, Astro prefetch, Rails with Turbo, Angular, Docusaurus, Inertia) aren't flagged: their links don't load a new document, and Turbo 8 already prefetches on hover. When most pages are built in the browser (a Vite or Create React App single-page app with a sitemap), the server HTML shows neither the links nor the router, so PERF-09 is listed under Limits instead.
- **Retired hint:** `prerender-hint` (low) flags `<link rel="prerender">`. Chrome treats it as NoState Prefetch, which fetches some of the next page's files but doesn't render it, and may retire it; speculation rules replace it (developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/prerender-pages, checked 21 Nov 2026).
- **Pass:** the shared layout prefetches same-site links once a visitor is about to open one (`"eagerness": "moderate"`: on hover or press), and leaves out links that change something (log out, add to cart). Prerendering is faster still but runs the next page's scripts and analytics early, so keep it to a few likely pages.
- **Fix:** `<script type="speculationrules">{"prefetch":[{"where":{"and":[{"href_matches":"/*"},{"not":{"href_matches":"/logout"}}]},"eagerness":"moderate"}]}</script>` in the layout, or a `Speculation-Rules` header pointing at a JSON file of the same rules. WordPress 6.8 and later adds rules itself, and some CDNs send the header as a setting. Chrome and Edge use the rules; other browsers ignore them.

### PERF-10 · Back and Forward are instant (back/forward cache) · low (medium when Chrome's own test fails)
> Checked **19 Nov 2026** against web.dev/articles/bfcache and developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/bfcache-ccns. Chrome now keeps pages sent with `Cache-Control: no-store` (rolled out from Chrome 116, planned for all users by April 2025), but drops them when a cookie changes, after 3 minutes, or when they use WebSocket, WebTransport or WebRTC; other browsers may still refuse them. Firefox refuses pages with an `unload` handler; Chrome has deprecated `unload`, but headless Chrome 157 still refused such a page.
- **Check:** on every indexable page the crawler flags `bfcache-no-store` (an HTML page sent with `Cache-Control: no-store`) and `bfcache-unload` (an `unload` listener in an inline script, `onunload =`, or an `onunload` attribute), both low. They're hints: external scripts aren't read, and browsers differ.
- **Real test:** with outside checks on and Chrome or Edge installed, each page in the render sample gets Chrome's own test, as DevTools runs it: load the page, go to the next sample page, press Back. `bfcache-blocked` (medium) lists Chrome's reasons when the page had to load again; reasons Chrome marks circumstantial (memory, the test itself) are left out. When it ran, a failed real test is the stronger evidence and the hints are leads. PERF-10 passes only when this test ran on at least one page: without it (outside checks off, no Chrome or Edge, a one-page sample, or a timeout on every page) a hint still raises its finding, and otherwise PERF-10 is listed under Limits with the reason.
- **Pass:** ordinary pages are sent with `no-cache` or `max-age=0`, keeping `no-store` for pages with private data. No `unload` handlers in the site's code or its third-party scripts: `pagehide`, or `visibilitychange` for analytics and saving state. Open connections (WebSocket, WebRTC) close on `pagehide` and reopen on `pageshow`.
- **Fix:** Chrome DevTools > Application > Back/forward cache > Test names each blocker. In Chromium browsers `Permissions-Policy: unload=()` also stops third-party scripts from adding `unload` handlers.
