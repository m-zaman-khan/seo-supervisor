# Changelog

## 2.0.0 (2026-11-18)

Deeper checks, optional real-world data and search intent. Everything still works with no keys, and nothing leaves the machine by default.

- **Since last audit.** `seo-diff.mjs` compares two crawls by path (so a local crawl compares with a public one), and the report gets a "Since last audit" tab: regressions first (noindex added, H1 or schema removed, canonical moved away, page now an error, title emptied), then improvements, other changes, and pages added or gone. The skill keeps earlier crawls in `seo-audit/history/`, and review mode compares against them.
- **New crawler checks:** hreflang (return links, self entry, x-default, valid codes, working targets), doctype, charset and viewport (REND-09), meta refresh, og:url against the canonical, robots meta against the X-Robots-Tag header, `max-image-preview:large`, image weight and legacy formats (PERF-08), merchant-listing schema for shops (Offer, shipping, returns, and the price shown on the page), Content-Signal values, Markdown negotiation without `Vary: Accept`, and the llms.txt format.
- **Live HTTPS checks** with `--check-live` (TRUST-07, URL-03): certificate trust, the hosts it covers, time left scaled to its lifetime, the http→https redirect and HSTS. A certificate blocker fails the launch gate.
- **Outside checks, off until the owner says yes** (`--outside`): real-user Core Web Vitals from PageSpeed Insights for up to 10 pages (PERF-01; needs a free `PSI_API_KEY`, since Google refuses keyless requests), plus the site's 25-week speed trend from the CrUX History API with `CRUX_API_KEY`, and a headless Chrome or Edge render of up to 10 pages compared with the raw HTML (REND-06). The answer is kept in `seo-audit/settings.json`. When it's off, these are listed under Limits.
- **Search Console import.** `gsc-import.mjs` reads the Performance export, including a BOM, quoted commas and percent CTRs. Findings and plan rows show their clicks, and fixes are ordered by traffic within each severity. Three new findings: a low click rate for the position (INTENT-03), almost on page one (INTENT-04), and pages competing for one query (INTENT-02, from per-page exports only).
- **Search intent and strategy.** New `rules/search-intent.md` (INTENT-01 to 05) and `strategy.md` (reading search results, topic clusters, the brief format). Intent findings name the search engine and date and are medium at most. Content gaps are the owner's decision, never new pages on their own.
- **New `/seo-brief` command.** It writes one content brief to `seo-audit/briefs/` and edits nothing. Setup's drafts now start with the same brief.
- **Content scan:** invisible characters, templated descriptions and unsourced statistics.
- **Score fix.** A missing title (or one title shared by most pages), H1 or canonical on at least 80% of indexable pages is now one site-wide blocker, so a site with no SEO scores below 60 instead of in the 80s.
- **Fetched content is data.** Text on a crawled page that addresses the agent is reported as a finding, never followed.
- **Share images made locally.** `og-card.mjs` makes 1200×630 Open Graph cards from plain HTML with the installed Chrome or Edge (one page or a batch), with no AI and no outside service, and checks the size before writing. `og-images.md` says when to use a card or a real photo, the sizes and tags Discover and Meta need, and to prefer the framework's own generator. The crawler now reads share images (same site only): broken, narrower than 1200 px, or missing width and height tags.
- **IndexNow.** `indexnow.mjs` creates the key file and tells Bing, Yandex, Seznam, Naver, Yep, Internet Archive and Amazonbot (not Google) which URLs changed, from a list, a diff or the sitemap. It's a dry run until the owner says yes, and checks the key file is live before sending (MAP-08).
- **Server log scan.** `log-scan.mjs` reads the site's own access logs locally: how search, AI search and AI training bots treat the site, the status codes they get, and visits referred by AI assistants. Findings: crawler 5xx errors, URLs that keep returning 404 to search crawlers, and AI search bots blocked by a firewall or CDN.
- **Structured data checker.** `schema-check.mjs` checks JSON-LD offline against schema.org 30.1 and Google's rich-result requirements, with line and column for errors. Opt-in triggers in `examples/hooks/`: pre-commit, GitHub Actions, and Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Copilot hooks.
- **More checks:** sitemap lastmod dates in the future or not in W3C format, and priority/changefreq tags Google ignores (MAP-04). "Since last audit" also compares descriptions, Open Graph tags and big word-count changes.
- **Phone and desktop checks.** Outside checks now load the sample pages at desktop and phone size in headless Chrome or Edge, driven over the DevTools pipe with no dependencies. They save screenshots to `seo-audit/screenshots/`, flag content missing on phones (REND-07), sideways scrolling and tiny text (REND-09) and full-screen popups (PERF-07), and add an indicative lab-speed line for sites that aren't live yet.
- **Node 22 or newer** is now required (Node 20 reached end of life on 30 April 2026). CI runs on Node 22, 24 and 26.
- **Connect Google Search Console and Analytics (optional, read-only).** `google.mjs` signs in with Google in the browser, or uses the owner's own Desktop client or a service-account key (`guide` has the steps). `fetch` pulls pages, queries and page-by-query data in the same shape as the CSV import, inspects up to 20 key pages (not indexed: CRAWL-02; Google chose a different canonical: URL-04), reads submitted sitemaps (errors: MAP-01) and adds GA4 organic sessions by landing page. The login stays in `~/.seo-supervisor/google.json`, readable only by the owner; `status` shows it and `disconnect` withdraws it at Google.
- **Key-only connections (optional).** `connect-keys.mjs` connects Bing Webmaster Tools (Bing clicks and impressions, linked pages, Bing's crawl problems), Open PageRank and Ahrefs' free Domain Rating (authority for the site and named competitors, shown on the Overview with Ahrefs' credit) with a pasted API key. Fetching is a dry run until `--yes`.
- **Update notices.** At most once a week the agent checks npm for a newer SEO Supervisor (only the package name is sent) and mentions it once, with the update command. It never updates by itself. Off with `update-check.mjs off`, `SEO_SUPERVISOR_NO_UPDATE_CHECK=1`, and in CI. The report footer now names the version that made it.
- **Lighthouse, optional.** The installer offers Lighthouse 13 as an add-on (about 170 MB in `~/.seo-supervisor/tools`, never in the project; `--with-lighthouse`, `--no-lighthouse`; a failed install never stops the skill install). With outside checks on, `--lighthouse` runs it on up to 5 sample pages with the installed Chrome or Edge: category scores, lab metrics and the costliest failed audits go into the crawl, plus one Overview line. Information only, no findings.
- **Submit your sitemap to Search Console (optional).** `google.mjs submit-sitemap` is a dry run until the owner says yes; only then does a second short Google screen ask for the one write permission (incremental authorization). It needs Owner or Full permission on the property.
- **The report shows every source per page:** Search Console clicks and impressions, Bing clicks and impressions, and Google Analytics organic visits on findings and plan rows (in the Markdown too), Bing and Analytics totals on the Overview, and Bing's most-linked pages with sample links.
- **LCP breakdown.** The Overview names the biggest of LCP's four parts (server response, the wait before the main image starts loading, its download, drawing it) with the fix for that part. It uses real visitors with `CRUX_API_KEY`, else the home page's lab run (PageSpeed or Lighthouse). Fixed: a CrUX week with no data no longer reads as "LCP 0.0 s" in the trend.
- **Since last audit, more comparisons:** pages that got slower or faster (real visitors, lab load and Lighthouse compared separately, each with its own threshold so noise isn't reported) and schema types that lost or gained fields (e.g. "Product lost offers").
- **New crawler checks:** next-page preloading (PERF-09); back/forward cache hints, plus Chrome's own back/forward test with outside checks (PERF-10); pages over 1,400 elements; links with no text; http:// internal links on HTTPS sites; titles that don't match their page (META-09); charset header and meta disagreeing; obsolete tags (REND-10).
- **Lighthouse on every page.** `--lighthouse all` runs it on every indexable page, up to 200, and prints the time estimate first. The report line adds the lowest-scoring pages.
- **Google ranking updates.** `google-updates.mjs` reads Google's public Search Status Dashboard and lists the ranking updates in the period, on the Overview and Since last audit, so an owner can tell a drop that came with a Google update from one the site caused. It sends nothing about the site, needs outside checks, and never claims an update caused anything.
- **Bing's AI preference switch (AI-03).** Bing honours its new `ai-grounding` and `ai-training` directives from 1 Feb 2027 and stops reading `noarchive` and `nocache` as AI controls on 1 Mar 2027. The crawl now flags indexable pages still on noarchive or nocache without the new directive (`ai-legacy-noarchive`, a decision), with Bing's dates and Amazon's "don't train" reading of noarchive, and values Bing doesn't list (`ai-preference-invalid`, low).
- **New AI crawlers.** The robots table adds MistralAI-Index, MistralAI-Training, MistralAI-User, DuckAssistBot, Amzn-SearchBot, Amzn-User, Amazonbot, Meta-ExternalFetcher, YandexAdditionalBot (or YandexAdditional) and Applebot, which follows the Googlebot group when it isn't named. The log scan adds those that visit, plus Claude-Agent, with updated purposes for Applebot, Amazonbot and Mistral, and `--verify-ips` reads DuckDuckGo's and Mistral's lists.
- **Shared AI-training opt-out.** The crawl reads `User-agent: __usage_ai-training` (an IETF draft; Anthropic honours it since 30 Oct 2026, Bing from 1 Feb 2027) as `trainingOptOut`, and the report says when it's missing that `User-agent: *` doesn't cover it.
- **Google visits in server logs.** `log-scan.mjs` counts landing pages from Google Search's documented referrers (Google, 12 Nov 2026), including the Android app's, as `googleReferrals`; files such as images aren't counted.
- **Search Console's brand label and Lens data.** `google.mjs fetch` adds `brandSplit`, Google's own brand vs non-brand split (`brandedClassification`, for properties Google labels), and Google Lens and Circle to Search results (`webMultimodal`) beside the other Google results.
- **Internal link ideas and searches a page doesn't mention.** The crawl now writes each indexable page's main-text words and links to `seo-crawl.words.json` next to `seo-crawl.json`. With Search Console's per-page queries, `keyword-ideas.mjs` lists two things for each page: the searches it shows for whose words its title, H1 and main text don't use, and other pages that mention one of its searches but don't link to it (up to 3 each, 50 at most: our own limits). Both are in `keyword-ideas.json` and `.md`, and in two report sections once copied into the audit. Information only: no findings, no score.
- **Did a change help? A before/after check with a control group.** `change-check.mjs` compares the Google Search clicks and impressions of the pages the owner changed on a date (`--pages <file>` or `--from-diff seo-audit/seo-diff.json`) with similar pages that weren't changed, over the same dates (28 days each side by default). It answers "likely up", "likely down" or "inconclusive", with a likely range. Comparing with unchanged pages takes out moves across the whole site, such as Google's smaller core updates, which Google doesn't announce. It reads Search Console with the existing Google sign-in, says how many days to wait until Search Console's numbers are final, and never claims a cause. Information only: one line in the report, never a finding or the score.
- **AI crawler rules that contradict each other.** `ai-robots-conflict` (MAP-06, needs-decision) names each case in a plain sentence: a vendor's AI search crawler allowed while its fetcher for users is blocked, or the reverse; a Content-Signal line that says the opposite of its group's Allow and Disallow lines, or of another line in the same group; and the shared training opt-out set while a training crawler's own group lets it in, or disagreeing with a Content-Signal `ai-train` value. Blocking training while allowing search isn't flagged.
- **More in the AI panel, information only:** aipref `Content-Usage` lines as written (`contentUsage`; IETF drafts, not final), a note when robots.txt carries Cloudflare's Bot Preference Sync block (`botPreferenceSync`), so the live file can differ from the one in the code, and Brave's crawler, which has no user agent of its own, won't crawl what Googlebot can't, and needs `noindex`, not robots.txt, to drop a page.
- **Clearer AI crawler probe.** `--ai-bot-probe` lists a 402 Payment Required (Cloudflare's pay per crawl, with its `crawler-price`) as paid access (`aiBotPaid`), not a refusal, and names Cloudflare's Bot Fight Mode as a likely cause when Cloudflare challenges the crawler.
- **Page checks.** The crawl flags links to other sites in comment sections without `rel="ugc"`, `nofollow` or `sponsored` (`ugc-link-followed`, CONTENT-11; Google's user-generated spam guidance, 26 Oct 2026), inline scripts that change the browser history on load and send visitors elsewhere on Back (`back-button-trap`, CONTENT-11, a possible issue; Google's back-button hijacking policy, enforced since 15 Jun 2026), paywalled pages whose paid section is already in the HTML (`paywall-text-sent`, REND-06; Google, 28 Aug 2025, with Apple's note that it doesn't use `isAccessibleForFree: false` pages as AI context), a title, description, canonical or robots tag sent after `</head>`, as Next.js's streamed metadata does (`head-tags-late`, REND-08), and pages whose Organization or WebSite `@id` or name differs from the one most pages use (`site-identity-differs`, SCHEMA-02). All low.
- **Pages with one incoming link.** The crawl lists pages that can be indexed with exactly one other page linking to them (`linkedOnce`: the count and the first 20, each with the page that links to it; the home page and self-links aside), and the report shows them in a collapsed "Pages with one incoming link" panel. Information only: never a finding or the score.
- **Links to a missing #section.** `link-fragment-missing` (CRAWL-07, low) is a link to `page#name` where the crawled page has no `id="name"` or `<a name="name">`. A bare `#`, `#top`, router addresses (`#/`, `#!`), text fragments, links that open tabs or menus, pages the crawl didn't read and pages built only in the browser are left out.
- **JavaScript changing the H1 or meta description.** The render comparison now reports a meta description that JavaScript changes, adds or removes, and an H1 from the raw HTML that JavaScript changes or removes, as `render-changes-text` (REND-06, low): Google says JavaScript may set the description, but crawlers that don't run JavaScript see the server's version. Title, canonical and robots changes stay `render-changes-head` (high), and an H1 that only JavaScript adds stays `render-adds-content` (REND-01).
- **The crawler slows down on 503s and dropped connections too.** Its own requests to the site now pause on a 503 (honouring Retry-After, 30 s at most) and for 2 s when a request gets no answer, then ask once more, as they already did for a 429. The pauses stop at 2 minutes in all (our own cap), and a note says when the crawl slowed down. Google's 11 Nov 2026 post on moving to a CDN names 429 and 503 as a site's temporary brake.
- **Hygiene checks.** The crawler flags the same Google Analytics 4 ID set up in two scripts, or one Tag Manager container loaded twice (`analytics-tag-twice`, LAUNCH-04, low); secret keys in a page's HTML: Stripe live secret and restricted keys, GitHub and Slack tokens and private keys, never browser keys such as Google's or Stripe's publishable ones (`secret-key-exposed`, TRUST-01, high, shown only by type and first characters); and three or more links to other sites hidden by an element's own style (`hidden-links`, CONTENT-11, low). Structured data: a nested review that names a different item in `itemReviewed` (`review-target-conflict`, SCHEMA-01) and online-only events, which Google's event results dropped in June 2025 (`event-online`, SCHEMA-05), in the crawler and `schema-check.mjs`. Information only: missing or broken app link files when the site promotes an app, and PDFs that many pages or the menu link to.
- **New searches this period.** `google.mjs fetch` lists the searches that showed the site this period but had no impressions in the period before, same length (`newQueries`, the top 50 by impressions). One quiet Overview line shows the first 10. Information only.
- **Markdown that drifted from the page.** When the site serves Markdown to agents, the crawler compares the start page's Markdown with its HTML and flags a big difference (`markdown-differs`, AI-05, low): under half the page's main words, or over half of the Markdown's words not on the page (our own limit).
- **YandexBot in `log-scan.mjs --verify-ips`.** Yandex publishes no IP list, so its requests are checked the way Yandex documents: a reverse DNS lookup must give a name on yandex.ru, yandex.net or yandex.com, and that name must lead back to the same address. The lookups go to the computer's own DNS server. `--verify-ips <folder>` contacts nothing, so it leaves YandexBot unchecked, with a note.
- **Check the fix yourself.** Findings a one-line command can prove (security headers, HSTS, redirects, robots.txt, canonicals, noindex) now show that command on the finding card and in the Markdown, filled with the finding's first URL: for example `curl -sI 'https://www.example.com/'`. The owner runs it; nothing runs it for them. A crawled URL that could break out of the quotes gets no command. On Windows, type `curl.exe` in PowerShell.
- **A quick check after each deploy.** `deploy-check.mjs --site https://www.example.com` (or `npx seo-supervisor deploy-check`) takes about ten seconds and exits 1 on a blocker, for CI: the home page answers 200 and isn't noindex, robots.txt doesn't block the site (or fail with a server error), the sitemap is listed and answers, the home page's canonical points to itself, and the certificate's days left. It only contacts the site itself. `--json` for machines.
- **Turn a check off, or change its severity, per project.** A `checks` section in `seo-audit/settings.json` (`{ "off": ["no-hsts"], "severity": { "img-alt-quality": "medium" } }`) applies to the report, the score, the dashboard and GitHub issues alike. The report always lists what's turned off or changed, and warns about unknown checks and severities. Blockers can't be turned off or lowered there.
- **Findings as GitHub issues, on your yes.** `issues.mjs --audit seo-audit/seo-audit.json [--ids F-001,F-002] [--repo owner/name]` (or `npx seo-supervisor issues`) prints each issue first; with `--yes` it files them with your own `gh` login and skips findings already filed. It never reads or stores a token.
- **Lighthouse's accessibility checks.** With the Lighthouse add-on, the report lists each page's failed Lighthouse accessibility checks and the page elements they name. They're Lighthouse's checks, information only, and don't change the score.
- **Internal link strength.** The crawl gives each page that can be indexed a 0–100 internal link strength (`linkStrength`: by path, plus the median; skipped on `--include`/`--exclude` crawls, flagged when stopped at `--max`). It's our own measure: the PageRank formula (Brin and Page, 1998; damping 0.85, redirects followed, each linking page counted once) on the site's own links, on a logarithmic scale where 100 is the strongest page. It's not Google's PageRank. The report shows it in a collapsed "Internal link strength" panel: the 10 strongest pages and, with Search Console traffic, the pages people find in search that get little link strength. Information only: never a finding or the score.
- **A missing llms.txt is now a low finding** (`llms-txt-missing`, MAP-07). It replaces the crawl note. llms.txt is a short Markdown map of the key pages that some AI tools and coding assistants read, so SEO Supervisor asks for one; it's never presented as a ranking fix (Google: it "will neither harm nor help" rankings). An HTML page at /llms.txt counts as missing, and `"checks": { "off": ["llms-txt-missing"] }` in `seo-audit/settings.json` turns it off.
- **Sourced notes in the rules:** Change of Address for every variant of an old domain (LAUNCH-01), the Indexing API's job and livestream scope (LAUNCH-02), unannounced smaller core updates and the September 2025 `num=100` impressions drop, labelled third-party (LAUNCH-08), pre-warming a new CDN (CRAWL-09), response headers inside Googlebot's 2 MB (REND-08), which crawlers honour Crawl-delay (MAP-05), Google's unnamed training token, Web Bot Auth, RSL and NLWeb (AI-02), Bing's grounding advice as reported (AI-04), Bing's Opportunity Queries and Google Search profiles (AI-06), third-party scores aren't Google metrics (AI myths), LCP is still 2.5 s, ratings with a comment and author (SCHEMA-04), and Business Agent, Microsoft Merchant Center and ChatGPT shopping (SHOP-08).
- **Google traffic in the deploy check (optional).** `deploy-check.mjs --search-console [--since <deploy time>]` reads your own Search Console's hourly data (Google added it to the API in April 2025, up to 10 days back) and shows Google Search clicks and impressions for the hours since the deploy beside the same hours a week before. It warns when clicks fell 30% or more (our own line) and says "too little data to tell" under 100 clicks a week before. Hourly data is fresh and noisy, so it never fails the check. It uses the `google.mjs connect` sign-in, and without one it prints how to connect and runs the rest as usual. `--json` adds a `searchConsole` block.
- **AI answer check (opt-in).** `ai-answers.mjs` asks Perplexity (its Sonar model, through the Agent API) and OpenAI (the model ChatGPT uses, with web search) up to 20 of the owner's questions, 3 times each by default, with the owner's own keys. It counts how many answers cited the site (www or not), each rival named, and which of the site's pages. The output is a sample, like "cited in 7 of 30 answers (10 questions, asked 3 times each, on 2026-11-22)", never a score, ranking, position or sentiment. Without `--yes` it's a dry run that prints every question, where it goes and how many requests that is; only the questions and the key are sent. A bad key, no credit or a rate limit gets a plain message, and the answers already collected are kept. `connect-keys.mjs` gets two optional keys, `perplexity` and `openai`, entered at the hidden prompt, with a `guide` for each. The report shows a collapsed "AI answer check" panel, information only.
- **Markdown checked on more pages.** The Markdown comparison (`markdown-differs`, AI-05, low) now covers the same sample of up to 10 pages as the Chrome render comparison: the home page and one page from each top folder. Other pages are asked for Markdown only when the start page answers with Markdown. The start page is asked once, not twice, and a site that serves no Markdown gets no extra requests. The limits and wording are the same as before.
- **Secret keys in script files.** `secret-key-exposed` (TRUST-01, high) now also reads the site's own JavaScript files, where a build often bakes in a server key. Only `<script src>` files on the crawled site are read, each once however many pages load it. Other hosts (CDNs, third parties) and source maps are never asked. The crawler's slow-down pauses and timeouts apply. Our own limits: 50 files, 5 MB each, 50 MB in all. A note says how many files weren't read. The evidence names the script and a page that loads it, and shows only the key's type and first characters. Browser keys stay unflagged.
- **Many new pages at once.** Since last audit now says when many new pages appeared together (our own line: at least 50, and at least 20% more than the last audit found). It gives the count, the new pages per folder and 10 examples taken from different folders, and points to Google's help on hacked content. It's information only, with no judgment of what the pages are about: a hacked site often grows hundreds of spam pages overnight, and a new folder full of pages is the plainest sign.
- **Geo-grid local rank check (opt-in).** `geo-grid.mjs` asks Google's Places API (New) for 1 to 3 keywords at each point of a 5, 7 or 9 point square grid around a local business (1 km apart by default), with the owner's own Google Maps Platform key, and records where the business's own place ID sits in the list Google returns there: 1 to 20, or not in the top 20. It asks for place IDs only, which Google's price list shows as free without limit, sends the bare keyword with a bias circle at each point, and keeps nothing but place IDs and positions, credited to Google Maps. It's an approximation of Google Maps results, not a copy. Without `--yes` it's a dry run that gives the number of requests, the free tier and the EEA note. `--find "Business name, town"` shows Google's matches and saves only the place ID picked. `connect-keys.mjs` gets an optional `googleplaces` key with a setup guide, and plain messages for a bad key, a turned-off API, missing billing, a rate limit and an out-of-date place ID.
- **Local rank grid in the report.** A collapsed "Local rank grid" panel draws each keyword's grid with no map underneath (Google's terms forbid a non-Google map): one square per spot, north at the top, the business's own square outlined, coloured with the report's red-to-green scale with the position written in every square, a legend, and "Places data: Google Maps" with any third-party credit Google returned. The counts are worked out from the squares, the label says it's an approximation of Google Maps results, and a grid with a missing square isn't drawn. Information only.
- **Microsoft Clarity (optional).** `connect-keys.mjs` reads what real visitors did on each page in Clarity's last 3 days: sessions, average scroll depth, and the share of sessions with quick backs, rage clicks, dead clicks and excessive scrolling. It shows under each finding's traffic line and as one Overview line naming the pages with the most quick backs and rage clicks. Offered only when the site uses Clarity. Only the token is sent (it names the project), one request a fetch of the 10 Clarity allows a day. A metric Clarity sends in another form is left out and named in a note, never guessed. Heat maps and recordings stay in Clarity. Information only.
- **Matomo and PostHog (optional).** `connect-keys.mjs` adds visits from search engines by landing page from either, in each page's traffic line and as Overview totals. Offered only when the site uses one; the token or key is typed at a hidden prompt and never goes in a URL (Matomo's in the request body, PostHog's in a header), and redirects aren't followed.
- **Known backlinks.** `backlinks.mjs` revisits the pages that link to the site (from Bing, a Search Console links export or a list) and reports which still link, which are gone (LAUNCH-05) and which couldn't be checked. Dry run until the owner says yes.
- **New guidance:** how to build fair "X vs Y" and "alternatives to X" pages (SVC-08), and no links to retired Google Business Profile features: chat, `business.site` websites and Q&A (LOCAL-10).
- **More from Search Console.** `google.mjs fetch` adds:
  - image, video, news and Discover results
  - clicks by device and country
  - the change from the period before, with the pages that dropped most
  - rich-result errors from URL Inspection (SCHEMA-08)
  - `--inspect`/`--inspect-urls` to choose the pages to inspect
  - a note when the period overlaps a data anomaly on Google's own page
- **Sitemaps.**
  - Gzipped sitemaps and indexes are read (up to 50 MB uncompressed), and so are text sitemaps.
  - New checks: sitemaps that aren't valid XML (`sitemap-invalid`, with line and column), files over 50,000 URLs or 50 MB (`sitemap-too-big`), and News sitemap rules.
  - A robots.txt answering 5xx, 429 or nothing is `robots-server-error` (CRAWL-01, high), not "missing".
- **Live checks.**
  - The other host (www or bare domain) must redirect to the preferred one in one permanent hop with the path kept (URL-03).
  - A failed HTTPS connection is a finding (TRUST-07).
  - Temporary redirects are flagged where a permanent one belongs.
  - Chrome's own error pages no longer raise false render findings.
- **Crawler fixes:**
  - robots `none` counts as noindex and nofollow.
  - X-Robots-Tag rules for other bots no longer count for Google.
  - `Vary: Accept` is matched exactly.
  - Chinese, Japanese and Thai pages are counted in words.
  - A missing llms.txt is a note, not a finding.
  - `lang` and `<base href>` are read on every page.
  - Every offer of a product is checked for shipping and returns.
  - Cloudflare's `use=` Content-Signal key is accepted.
  - Book Actions is no longer listed as retired.
- **New crawler checks:**
  - noindex with a canonical to another page (CRAWL-02)
  - page-level nofollow (META-06)
  - Microdata and RDFa recognised, with JSON-LD suggested (SCHEMA-01)
  - favicon for search results (META-10)
  - render-blocking scripts in the head (PERF-03)
  - a local business's phone and address shown on the page (LOCAL-05)
  - what Googlebot gets compared with what a visitor gets (CRAWL-09). A firewall that blocks requests only claiming to be Googlebot is a decision for the owner, not a finding, since that is the right setup when it verifies Google by IP.
  - product images under Google's 50,000-pixel minimum (SHOP-05)
- **Local, shop and links guidance:**
  - Business Profile category and completeness (LOCAL-11, LOCAL-12)
  - Bing Places and Apple Business (LOCAL-13)
  - trade directories (LOCAL-14)
  - steady reviews (LOCAL-09)
  - Merchant Center free listings (SHOP-08)
  - earning links honestly (LINK-10)
  - what to check when security warnings appear (LAUNCH-05)
- **No word-count targets:** thin-page is a page to look at, not a word goal, and posts and city intros carry no word counts. LAUNCH-08 no longer lists update dates from memory.

- **Entities in titles are measured right.** The crawler decodes the named references titles use (`&ndash;`, `&rsquo;`, `&hellip;`, accented letters such as `&eacute;`) and numeric ones, with one decoder and one attribute parser for every page check, so "Pricing &ndash; Acme" is no longer counted 6 characters too long.
- **Headings, short pages and long titles follow Google's own words.** `h1-count` is now two low findings, `h1-missing` and `h1-multiple`, and a settings.json that turns `h1-count` off turns off both. `heading-skip` is low and says heading order matters to screen readers, not to Google Search. `thin-page` and `title-long` are low: Google sets no word count and no title length limit, and 150 words and 60 characters are our own lines.
- **Orphan pages are split.** `orphan` (medium) is a sitemap page no other page links to; a page linked only from menus, footers or sidebars is the new low `nav-only-links`. LINK-01 is low in its rule, as the crawler already had it.
- **Near-duplicates check every page,** not only the first 400, and faster: on the 2,000-page benchmark the step takes 0.7 s for all pages, against 1.9 s for the first 400 before.
- **How to tell it worked.** `grow.md` has a row for each rule area: the number to watch, where to see it, which way it should move and when. Each grow-plan item and each fixed finding gets a Watch line.
- **Business types come from schema.org's type tree.** One test (`scripts/lib/business.mjs`) decides what counts as business schema. A home page marked up as a Bakery, HairSalon, MedicalClinic or any other Organization subtype no longer gets `org-missing`, and the address check (LOCAL-05), store-wide shipping and returns, site identity and the trust view read them too. A bare `Service` no longer counts as the site's organization.
- **Site identity (SCHEMA-02).** A relative `@id` such as `#organization` is read against the page it's on, so it no longer passes as one node on every page.
- **Self-serving ratings.** New `rating-self-serving` (SCHEMA-04, low): a rating or review of the site's own business in its schema. Google's review snippet guide makes those ineligible for stars.
- **Return policies.** `schema-check.mjs` asks for `merchantReturnDays` with a finite return window and `returnShippingFeesAmount` with a return shipping fee. An Organization's return policy needs `applicableCountry` with `returnPolicyCategory`, or `merchantReturnLink`, as Google's return policy doc sets them.
- `schema-check.mjs` reports `review-target-conflict` under SCHEMA-01, as the crawler does. Its shared checks moved to `scripts/checks/schema.mjs`, and `schema-check.mjs` still exports them.
- LOCAL-03 no longer gives a target number of city mentions: Google's spam policies call blocks of city names keyword stuffing.
- **One AI bot list.** `scripts/data/ai-bots.json` now drives the robots.txt table, the `ai-bots-blocked` finding, `log-scan.mjs`'s bots and IP lists, and the `--ai-bot-probe` user agents, so they can't disagree. `ai-bots-blocked` names each bot the table shows as blocked by its own group (or the vendor's second name, such as `YandexAdditional`), so blocking `MistralAI-Index` is now flagged. Anthropic's old `anthropic-ai` and `Claude-Web` names, which its crawler page no longer lists, are gone. Mistral is "Mistral AI" everywhere.
- **New AI bots:** Meta's `Meta-WebIndexer` (Meta AI search) and `Meta-ExternalAds` (advertising), from Meta's crawler page. `YandexAdditionalBot` reads only a group that names it, as Yandex's robot table says, so `User-agent: *` no longer shows it as blocked.
- **AI search wording:** blocking Google-Extended doesn't remove a site from AI Overviews or AI Mode (AI-02 and the `ai-bots-blocked` text, citing Google's Search generative AI control page). Preferred sources need the site included in Search generative AI features, and only domains and subdomains count (AI-06). The rendering rule no longer says most AI crawlers don't run JavaScript: their makers don't document it.
- **Render comparison names what JavaScript adds.** It reports the JSON-LD types and the words that appear only after JavaScript, so swapped text or a swapped schema type is caught, not only counts. A title that JavaScript sets or changes is now the low `render-changes-text`, because Google's JavaScript SEO basics say that's fine; canonical and robots changes stay high.
- **One REND-01 finding per page:** a page reported `client-rendered` no longer also gets `render-adds-content`.
- **lazy-lcp uses Chrome's own LCP check.** On pages a PageSpeed or Lighthouse lab run loaded, it follows Chrome's answer instead of guessing from the first image (often a logo). New low `lcp-not-discoverable`: the LCP image starts loading only from CSS or a script.
- **Font swap warning.** PERF-03 says `font-display: swap` can make text jump, and suggests `optional` or a size-adjusted fallback (web.dev).
- **HSTS.** A missing Strict-Transport-Security header is `no-hsts` with or without `--check-live`, and its max-age, subdomains and preload are read once. The HSTS findings no longer point at the preload list, which hstspreload.org doesn't recommend; the one-year line is labelled our own, and the fixes raise max-age in stages.
- **Report-Only CSP.** A `Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only` header alone no longer counts as a CSP: it blocks nothing.
- **Fixed: `server-known-holes` can be turned off in settings.json** without an "isn't a check" warning; `image-missing` and `adult-unlabelled` there now mean `product-image-missing` and `product-adult-unlabelled`.
- **Old check names keep working.** A check that's renamed keeps its old name in settings.json and in older audits: the report applies the setting to the new name and asks you to rename it.
- **Rule titles match the rule docs** (META-03 is "All metadata comes from one helper").
- **Findings read the same in every script:** "Found on" or "N cases, for example", with web addresses as code.
- **Safer crawler output.** Text from the site in notes (robots.txt lines, addresses, URLs) is quoted and cut to 80 characters, and every note and not-checked reason has secrets and keys hidden.
- **Exit codes agree.** When Google says no (no access, a quota, a refused sign-in) or can't be reached, `google.mjs` and `change-check.mjs` exit 1 and say so on stdout, as `connect-keys.mjs` does; bad input is still 2. `connect-keys.mjs status` exits 1 when no key is set, as `google.mjs status` does when nothing is connected.
- **Private files, honestly.** `keys.json` and `google.json` are made readable only by your user account (on Windows through the file's own access list), and the message says so only when that worked.
- **A key typed on the command line:** `connect-keys.mjs set <service> <key>` still works, but warns that the key stays in your shell's history.
- **Search Console:** `--days` must be a whole number from 1 to 480 in `google.mjs` (it rounded 90.7 to 91), as in `connect-keys.mjs`. The brand and non-brand split is compared with the period before, with a note when other searches fell while brand searches hid it.
- **Traffic by page.** Tracking parameters (`utm_*`, ad click IDs, Merchant Center's `srsltid`) and the other trailing slash no longer split a page's numbers: in `google.mjs fetch` and `change-check.mjs`, and in the report's Search Console, Bing, analytics and Clarity lines on findings.
- **Safer report links.** Only a full `http://` or `https://` address becomes a link in the report, on the page and in the Markdown: evidence, the site's address, the authority score's credit, Google's update and Search Central links, and plan drafts (a draft may also be a plain path next to the report). A `javascript:`, `data:` or `//host` address shows as text. The page also carries a strict Content-Security-Policy: only its own two scripts run, and it loads nothing from anywhere.
- **Markdown escaping.** Everything the Markdown report takes from the audit file is escaped with one set of rules: notes, not-checked reasons, passed checks, decisions, why and fix, fix summaries, the one-line summaries, paths, the site's name and address, and the plan. Text quoted from a page can't become a link, an image or a tag. The agent's own `code` and **bold** stay. GitHub issues use the same rules, so bold now shows there too.
- **Dates.** A date like 2026-11-17 now shows as that day on every computer; west of UTC it used to show the day before. Other times show in UTC. This holds in the report and the dashboard.
- **Lighthouse, once.** A failed accessibility audit is listed only under "Lighthouse's accessibility checks", not also in "Lighthouse details".
- **Dashboard.** It uses the report's own score summary, so its scores always match the reports.
- **render-report.** The audit file can come anywhere among the options (`--out x.html audit.json` works). A misspelt option stops it. A missing or broken input file gives one plain line and exit code 2. A failed PDF exits 1.
- **Sturdier report.** One bad URL in the lab speed, LCP or Lighthouse data no longer stops the whole report.
- **Inside the report.** Each Overview panel is drawn from one view model, so its page and Markdown versions can't drift. "How this score works" is written once, and a dead CSS rule is gone.
- **Fixed before release** (from the round's final review):
  - An off-site answer compressed with gzip, deflate or br that stalls or drops halfway now fails once the request's time is up, instead of hanging the crawl.
  - Image, stylesheet, script, icon and sitemap reads wait out the crawler's slow-down and ask again after a 429 or 503, like page reads. A file still busy after that is noted as not checked, never reported as broken, and a rate-limited sitemap's URLs are no longer lost.
  - Sitemap files are read 2 at a time at most (our own limit), so huge sitemaps can't hold 1 GB of memory, and agent files (`/.well-known/…`) are read up to 1 MB once unpacked (our own limit).
  - Site text in the sitemap, hreflang-target and jump-link notes is quoted and cut short, like the other notes.
  - A site whose home page address redirects (for example `/` to `/en/`) gets its home page checks again: Organization and WebSite schema, and the favicon.
  - `--help` and `-h` print each script's usage and exit 0 again.
  - The secret hider no longer hides crawled addresses that only look like keys (such as `/blog/sk-guide-…`).
  - A crawl that reads no pages (a site that's down) reports the plain "No XML sitemap found", not the small, well-linked site's version.
  - In an audit made before this version, an "exactly one H1" finding reads as "no H1" or "more than one H1" by its pages, so a setting for either applies.
- The schema checker's vocabulary comes from Schema.org (CC BY-SA 3.0); see `NOTICE`.

## 1.0.0 (2026-11-18)

First public release.

- **Modes:** `/seo-audit` (read-only audit and report), `/seo-fix` (fixes with re-checks), `/seo-review` (SEO regressions in a diff or PR) and `/seo-launch` (pass/fail gate plus launch-day steps).
- **Rules:** 149 rules across crawling and indexing, URLs and canonicals, rendering, metadata, content, structured data, sitemaps and robots, internal linking, performance, trust and security, AI search, media and launch monitoring. There are extra rules for tools, local business, content, services and e-commerce sites, and guides for Next.js, Rails and SPAs. Facts were checked on 16–17 Nov 2026.
- **Crawler:** zero-dependency, run against a local production build. It covers status codes, canonicals, noindex, titles, headings, JSON-LD, sitemaps, robots.txt, orphans, soft 404s, near-duplicates, redirect chains, security headers and banned or unconfirmed claims.
- **Content scanner:** finds templated copy, reused FAQs, missing fields and unpublished items in JSON, CSV or Markdown content before it becomes pages.
- **Report:** one offline HTML file, dark by default with a light theme switch. It shows a 0–100 health score with area scores, "what's moving your score", the score fixing the blockers unlocks, and "Needs you" labels on owner-only items. It has tabs for findings, decisions, changes, passed rules and limits, and comes with a full Markdown twin.
- **Safety:** an audit never edits anything. Fix mode never edits CMS content, never hides, removes or noindexes pages, and never changes production indexing without the owner's go-ahead.
- **Install:** as a Claude Code plugin, through `npx seo-supervisor`, or by copying the folder.
